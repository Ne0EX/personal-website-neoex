*The first thing I built was a blog. The last thing it needed to be was public.*

I had a Notion page, then a Substack, then a WordPress installation I never updated past 2021. Each of them asked the same implicit question every time I opened them: *are you ready to publish?* The answer was almost always no. So the drafts sat. So the ideas calcified into something I neither published nor revisited.

This is not a blog. It does not ask if I am ready to publish.

---

A digital garden is a place where things grow at their own pace, in their own direction, without a publication date forcing the shape. An entry here can be a seed — a paragraph that holds a single, half-formed observation. It can be a refined note with a stable method behind it. It can be ongoing, something I return to and revise as my thinking changes. The status lives in the frontmatter, visible to anyone who looks. I am not pretending these are finished.

What I am trying to keep openly: the architecture of how I think, more than the conclusions I arrive at. The process of tuning something — a pour-over recipe, a startup decision, a theory about taste — is more honest than the polished output. The polished output hides the dead ends.

What stays in the drawer: anything that involves other people without their consent, anything from work that carries confidentiality, anything that is still too raw to be useful to someone who isn't me. The rule is not privacy for its own sake. The rule is: if I would not say it to a thoughtful stranger in a coffee shop, it does not belong here yet.

---

The coordinate for this entry is San Francisco, where I first wrote it down. That is not where most of these thoughts came from. That is where I found the right shape for them.

The worldline metaphor, if it helps: the archive is not a timeline. It is a body in space, with thoughts hovering in orbit and photographs pinned to the surface below. New entries do not erase old ones. The older ones do not gain authority from age. The body just keeps accumulating.

I am not trying to be impressive here. I am trying to keep a legible record of what I paid attention to, and why, and what I changed my mind about.
