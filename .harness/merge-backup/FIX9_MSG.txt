fix(theme): invert dark-globe land polarity — light-on-dark continents

The real cause of "can't see the continents in dark" was contrast polarity, not
strength (per a perceptual-contrast analysis Peat shared, and his own eyes).
Light mode draws land DARK on a light sphere (dark-on-light — what human vision
reads best). Dark mode kept the same polarity: land darkened (multiply) on an
already-dark sphere = dark-on-dark, near-zero luminance separation = invisible
continents. Raising coastlineAlpha only made land darker = worse.

Fix: in dark mode invert the land encoding. The Earth specular image (ocean
bright, land dark) is inverted (land→bright, ocean→dark), tinted to a mid-teal
(landTint #7AA8BC), and `lighten`-composited onto the base so continents are
raised LIGHTER than the ocean (light-on-dark) while the ocean keeps the base
gradient. Continents now read by luminance without squinting. Light mode keeps
its original dark-on-light multiply (untouched). Pairs with the matte dark
sphere (no specular) from the prior fix.

Verified via live dev (chrome-devtools): dark continents clearly legible as
lighter landmasses; light mode unchanged.
