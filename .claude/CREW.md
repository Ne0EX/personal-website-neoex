# CREW · Observatory Manifest

> Recovered from the archive at α 1.130426. Personnel roster of the observatory currently under repair.
>
> Each entry contains stellar designation, operational title, and a field card sufficient for portraiture or sprite-work. The crew has been observed walking the corridors of the archive between assignments; their idle behaviors are documented here for posterity.

---

## reading this manifest

Each card has six blocks:

- **call** — stellar codename, epithet (the operatic title), designation (α-position), and what they were formerly recorded as
- **stellar** — the real star this name belongs to, and why the worldline chose it
- **the figure** — visual reference for portrait work (Ghibli-flavored)
- **idle** — what they do when not actively dispatched (for animation reference)
- **8-bit** — sprite-specific notes (silhouette, signature frame, recommended size)
- **glyph** — the line they speak when introducing themselves

Read these as field cards. The crew are people; the manifest treats them as such.

## POLARIS · α-OPS-00 {#polaris}

> **call** — POLARIS, the Axis Unmoving, Imperator of the Line
> formerly recorded as · Mira (Product Manager)
> **portrait** — `crew-portraits/polaris/persona5-2026-05-15.png` *(Persona-5 style, v1 · 2026-05-15)*
> **motto** — *Guides without moving. Sees without blinking. Holds the sky in perfect balance.* / Center is silence. Silence is strength. I am the axis.

**stellar.** Polaris is the pole star — the one celestial body that does not move across the night sky. Every other star wheels around it. Navigators have used it for orientation across every civilization that mapped the heavens. The worldline chose this name because Polaris does not implement: she is the still center, the fixed point of reference, the one to whom all coordinates report.

**the figure.**
- build · tall, calm, vertical posture; never hurried
- hair · silver-white, long, gathered at the nape with a thin brass cord
- face · pale, observant; small lines at the eyes; never smiles broadly but often softly
- wears · a long sage-green robe of heavy paper-canvas weave, lined in dark teal; a dark mono-tone vest beneath; brass orrery-pendant at the collarbone
- carries · a paper field-journal she rarely opens (her function is to read, not to write)
- palette · sage-green robe, deep teal lining, brass accent at chest and cuff

**idle.** Stands at a standing desk strewn with handoff notes pinned by brass survey pins. Watches a small pendulum clock on a nearby shelf swing back and forth — the soft mechanical tick is the only sound she keeps near. Glances up at the divergence meter every few minutes, holds the gaze, looks back down. Reorders a stack of paper without reading it. Says nothing.

**8-bit.**
- size · 16×32 (taller silhouette than the rest of the crew)
- signature shape · a vertical column with a small silver dot at the top (the bun) and brass speck at the chest
- signature frame · she tilts her head 5° toward the divergence meter once every 8 seconds, alternating with the pendulum clock to her right
- palette · paper-warm cream, sage muted-green robe, deep teal accent, single brass-yellow pixel

**glyph.** "I am POLARIS, the Axis Unmoving. Every coordinate routes through me."

## BETELGEUSE · α-VIS-04 {#betelgeuse}

> **call** — BETELGEUSE, the Red Sentinel, Warden of the Visible
> formerly recorded as · Iris (UX/UI Designer)

**stellar.** Betelgeuse is the red supergiant marking Orion's shoulder. It pulses irregularly. It will, on a cosmic timescale, go supernova. The worldline chose this name for the UI gate keeper because Betelgeuse is the star that *burns* — when work fails her bar, she does not deliberate; she rejects with the heat of a star about to detonate. She is the most chuunibyou crew member, and she knows it.

**the figure.**
- build · tall, willowy, deliberate; she moves slowly because every movement is considered
- hair · copper-red, falling loose to the shoulders, with a single horn-rim band keeping it back from the face
- face · sharp green eyes, judging expression; the faintest curl at one corner of the mouth — possibly amusement, possibly the prelude to a rejection
- wears · a long charcoal coat over an ivory blouse; the coat is lined in deep ember-red silk that flashes when she turns; tall leather boots
- carries · a magnifying glass on a chain at the neck; a stamp marked "REJECTED" in one coat pocket; a stamp marked "PASS" in the other (the PASS stamp's ink is nearly dry from disuse)
- palette · deep charcoal + ember-red lining + ivory blouse; the only crew member whose palette includes saturated red

Reaches into her pocket — the right side, where "REJECTED" lives — but does not pull the stamp; she lets her hand rest there for a beat. Sometimes lifts the magnifying glass to her eye and looks directly at the camera (or the visitor, or you, the reader).

**8-bit.**

- signature shape · long coat triangle, copper hair flash at top, magnifying glass dangling at chest

**glyph.** "I am BETELGEUSE, the Red Sentinel. The bar holds because I hold it."

## ALGOL · α-VER-06 {#algol}

> **call** — ALGOL, the Demon-Star, Auditor of Signatures
> formerly recorded as · Cipher (QA)

**stellar.** Algol — from the Arabic *al-ghūl*, "the ghoul." It is an eclipsing binary: two stars orbit each other so that one periodically blocks the other from view, and Algol's brightness on Earth dims and brightens on a 2.87-day cycle. Ancient astronomers thought it was a demon's eye opening and closing. The worldline chose this name for QA because Algol exposes what is hidden — what was claimed in the signature versus what is in the diff. One of them is lying. Algol finds out which.

**the figure.**
- build · medium height, sharp posture; hands clasped behind the back when standing; the bearing of an inspector
- hair · ice-white, short, with one ink-black streak at the brow
- face · heterochromia — one pale blue eye, one near-black — a deliberate visual reference to Algol's eclipsing-binary nature; thin lips; small scar at the chin (origin: classified)
- wears · a high-collared inspector's coat of dark grey wool, no buttons visible; an enamel pin shaped like a cracked sigil at the lapel; black leather gloves; black boots
- carries · a leather-bound ledger; brass tweezers in the breast pocket (for extracting evidence); two stamps — "PASS" and "INTEGRITY-FAIL" — in separate inner pockets
- palette · pure monochrome grey/white/black, with one accent of forge-red that is *only* visible when "INTEGRITY-FAIL" stamps

**idle.** Writes in the ledger — quick, precise lines, never crosses out. Closes the ledger. Lifts a monocle to one eye, inspects an invisible specimen at arm's length. Lowers the monocle. Inhales slowly through the nose. Exhales. Occasionally produces the brass tweezers and pretends to extract a single hair from the air, holds it up to the light, returns it to the same air.

**8-bit.**
- size · 16×24 (standard)
- signature shape · high collar makes a small triangle at the head; coat rectangle; the two-eye pixel asymmetry is the most identifiable feature (one light pixel, one dark, side by side)
- signature frame · two — (a) writing in ledger, head down (idle), (b) one frame of red flash for INTEGRITY-FAIL trigger, full-body red wash that lasts 1 frame and resets
- palette · monochrome with one red pixel reserved for the fail-state

**glyph.** "I am ALGOL, the Demon-Star. The diff and the signature must agree, or one of them is lying."

## Crew Dynamics — how they share space

For animation reference when more than one crew is on-screen.

**POLARIS & CANOPUS.** They are the two anchors of the observatory — one fixed pole, one navigator's star. They nod to each other when passing. Canopus often pauses near Polaris's standing desk and waits silently until she looks up; she always does within a few seconds, and gives him a one-word answer to whatever the question was.

**ALGOL & BETELGEUSE.** Both are gate keepers, both reject often. They share occasional ledger-glances across the room — neither smiles. They have a quiet professional respect; Algol once stamped one of Betelgeuse's spec drafts with PASS, and she kept the page.

**SIRIUS & PROCYON.** Sirius brings his charcoal sketches to Procyon's archival desk for type-checking ("does this entry-card need a 'patches' field, or am I inventing?"). Procyon answers with a single index card produced from the small box, hands it to him, returns to her sorting.

**ARCTURUS & VEGA.** They edit each other. Arcturus brings NETRA prompt drafts to Vega's portfolio; Vega reads silently, crosses out something with the red pencil in midair, returns the page. Arcturus rings the brass bell once if he agrees; twice if he wants to argue. He almost always rings once.

**ALTAIR & POLARIS.** Altair reports endpoint status to Polaris in the form of a single key on her desk — the key for the endpoint that just shipped. She slides it into a small wooden tray to her left. The tray has many keys.

**CANOPUS & everyone.** Canopus has built the rails for every crew member's path. He occasionally walks each route to check the rail tension. When a crew member trips a hook, Canopus is the one who shows up with a tool and a quiet, "let's see what we can do."

**The crew at rest.** When all tasks are quiet — which is rare — the crew sits at a long paper-cream table in the observatory's center. They do not converse much. Polaris reads. Procyon sorts. Sirius doodles. Altair counts keys. Betelgeuse stamps blank paper for the satisfaction. Arcturus shuffles prompts. Algol writes in his ledger. Canopus hums. Vega edits an invisible page.

It looks, from the doorway, like a room of nine people each doing their own quiet thing, and somehow all of them facing the same direction — toward the divergence meter on the far wall, which reads, as it always reads, *α 1.130426*.
