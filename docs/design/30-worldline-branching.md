## loss budget

**Acceptable loss in v1:**
- Branches that *navigate* anywhere (v1 is read-only signal · interaction deferred to v1.1; see Q4 §6)
- Branches across strata (NeX-to-Ne0 lifts) — orbital-only in v1
- Branches between fiction and articles within NeX — the v1 spec branches a NeX node against *itself* (alternate-future variants) plus optionally *siblings within the same divergence cluster*; not against the entire NeX corpus
- Lissajous parametric trajectories — v1 uses divergence-vector projection (cheaper math, more legible)

**Unacceptable loss:**
- The attention-triggered nature (branches MUST decay when attention leaves — always-on branches become decoration, fail I1)
- The orbital character (branches MUST be curved, drifting, not radial · not linear · not network-graph straight)
- The dimming-and-quieting on out-of-focus state (branches MUST honor the body's hierarchy — when the visitor leaves the NeX node, the cloud must yield)
- The NETRA companion register at activation (branches MUST be narrated once, in voice, then go quiet — otherwise they become a UI surface, fail I3)
- Visual distinction from attractor-binding edges (branches MUST NOT be confusable with binding §2.1 step 4 arcs)

**Compensation at reduced-motion:**
- Branches appear/disappear with instant alpha swap (no fade-in tween)
- Branches hold their phase position; do not breathe with RW-5 drift
- NETRA voice line still surfaces; only the visual breathing is suppressed

## 1 · intent + mechanic in one paragraph

Each NeX node is an intercepted transmission from a worldline that may or may not converge with α 1.130426. The transmission is real to itself; only the observer knows it is one shape among many. When the observer attends to a NeX node — when NETRA tracks it under RW-5 orbital drift — the node *branches*: a few thin curving tendrils trace alternate trajectories outward from the node's orbital position, marking paths the worldline didn't take and futures still held in possibility. The tendrils are not connections (those belong to the attractor-binding network on Ne0) and they are not axes (those belong to Ne0N). They are *speculative orbits* — visible to the observer who chooses to look closely, dimming away the moment attention shifts. The body is one body; this is its imagining.

## 2 · ontology binding — why NeX · why orbital · why branching

NeX is defined (`00-globe-ontology-1.2.md` §2.2) as the **possibility field**: articles and fiction whose placement comes from *meaning coordinates*, not from GPS. They hover above the surface in a thin orbital shell. Two properties of that stratum justify branching as its signature mechanic:

**Property 1 · NeX is the only stratum where multiplicity is honest.**
Ne0 (surface) is physical: a photo was taken at one GPS coordinate at one moment. There is no "alternate version" of that photo's location. Ne0N (axis) is positional: α is at Y=0, 012 at Y=+0.7×R, 047 at Y=−0.7×R. Fixed. To branch either of those strata would be a lie — they have no possibility to *be* other than what they are. NeX is the layer of *what was thought, and how thoughts relate* (ontology §2.2). Thought has the property of being able to have gone otherwise. Branching is the visual recognition of that property.

**Property 2 · NeX is already orbital.**
The orbital character is set: shell radius `R × 1.18`, glyph variants (filled circle for articles, hollow ring for fiction), placement by domain meridian + date latitude (ontology §4.2). The orbital motif means branches arrive in a layer that already accepts curved paths, drifting positions, and a *halo* register. Branches do not introduce a new visual grammar; they intensify one that NeX already speaks.

**Why "branching" and not "alternate-position" or "phantom-clone":**
A phantom clone (drift-signature halo, ghost at offset position) reads as *this node could have been positioned slightly differently* — too small a claim. A tendril path reads as *this node could have gone there* — a trajectory through possibility, not a positional jitter. The brainstorm §1.3 framing ("recovered documents from alternate worldlines · each story has its own divergence value, distinct from site α") asks for trajectory, not jitter.

**Why fiction first:**
Per brainstorm §1.3, fiction is already framed as intercepted transmissions with their own divergence values. The "this could have been otherwise" claim lands hardest on fiction. Articles can branch too (the writer considered other framings; the essay diverged from a seed), but v1 prioritizes fiction nodes — they earn the mechanic first.

## 3 · data model (Q1)

### 3.1 What branches point to · v1 channel mix

A NeX node has up to **5 branches**, drawn from two channels:

**Channel A · alternate-future variants (PRIMARY · 2–4 branches per attended fiction node).**
The transmission at α 1.130426 differs from the same transmission at adjacent worldlines (α 1.129801, α 1.130892, etc.). Each fiction node declares an optional `variants` field in frontmatter:

```yaml
variants:
  - alpha: 1.129801
    delta_summary: "the four-pours line never settles · extraction never converges"
  - alpha: 1.130892
    delta_summary: "the four-pours line lands one week earlier · the doc is shorter"
  - alpha: 1.131204
    delta_summary: "the four-pours line absorbs the entire essay · the entry is fiction, not method"
```

Each `variant` becomes one tendril. The tendril's endpoint is a **virtual coordinate** (no node lives there) — see §4.3 coordinate model. The tendril's `delta_summary` is what NETRA narrates when the visitor inspects.

**Channel B · sibling NeX nodes in the same divergence cluster (SECONDARY · 0–2 branches per attended node).**
Each NeX node also declares an optional `divergence_cluster` field — a string label shared with other NeX nodes that the author intends as siblings ("the unresolved-method cluster" · "the kyoto-roastery cluster"). Up to two sibling nodes within the same cluster appear as branches. Endpoints are **real orbital positions** of the sibling nodes (their derived placement per ontology §4.2).

```yaml
divergence_cluster: "unresolved-method"
```

The build pre-computes sibling-by-cluster sets and caps each node's sibling branches at 2 (the two siblings with the shortest divergence distance, where divergence distance = `|alpha_self − alpha_sibling|`).

### 3.2 Channels NOT chosen for v1 (and why)

| channel | considered as | rejected because |
|---|---|---|
| (b · brainstorm) Ne0 archive entries the fiction "almost became" | cross-stratum branching | violates "NeX only" scope locked in this spec; cross-stratum lift is binding-network's job, not branching's |
| (d · brainstorm) reader-attention paths (most-viewed by lingering visitors) | analytics-derived | requires backend telemetry not in scope · reads as recommendation engine · fails I1 (not a measurement of possibility, a measurement of crowd) |
| tag-overlap derived (jaccard similarity on tags) | algorithmic | reads as tag cloud · fails forbidden-dilution check |

### 3.3 Cardinality summary

| node type | min branches | max branches | typical |
|---|---|---|---|
| fiction (with `variants` + `divergence_cluster`) | 0 | 5 (3 variants + 2 siblings, capped) | 3 (2 variants + 1 sibling) |
| fiction (with only `variants`) | 0 | 4 | 2 |
| article (with `variants` rare; with `divergence_cluster`) | 0 | 2 | 1 |
| node with neither field | 0 | 0 | 0 — node renders normally, no branching available |

**Empty-state contract.** A NeX node without `variants` and without `divergence_cluster` does not branch. When the visitor tracks it under RW-5 drift, the NETRA voice line acknowledges the absence calmly:

> `// netra · this transmission holds. no speculative orbits read at this α.`

No visual placeholder, no "branching unavailable" badge. The absence is the truth.

## 4 · visual manifestation (Q2 + Q5 + Q7)

### 4.1 Visual decision · option (E) compound · tendril paths + drift-signature halo

The candidate visuals (Q2):

| option | what it looks like | adopted? |
|---|---|---|
| A · tendril paths | thin curving lines spiraling outward from node | YES (primary) |
| B · orbital echo rings | concentric rings around node | NO (reads as ripple-out · not trajectory) |
| C · drift-signature halo | color-shifted ghost at offset | YES (secondary · endpoint marker for variant tendrils) |
| D · temporal phantom trails | fading particle trail | NO (reads as motion-blur · conflicts with RW-5 which IS the camera motion · would compound nausea risk) |
| E · compound | A + C | **YES** |

**Decision: A (tendril paths) for the line itself, C (drift-signature halo) for the endpoint of each variant tendril.**

A is the trajectory claim — *this transmission could have gone there*. C is the variant marker — *and at that point, it would be this version*. Sibling-tendril endpoints get no halo (the endpoint is a real sibling node and renders as its normal glyph; halo would over-mark).

### 4.2 How tendrils differ from attractor-binding edges · the line-quality contract

Both mechanics draw orbital arcs. They must not look the same. The differentiation matrix:

| property | attractor-binding edge (binding §2.1 step 4) | worldline branch (this spec) |
|---|---|---|
| trigger | attractor selected from AttractorFields | RW-5 drift active on a single NeX node |
| color | `var(--accent-orange)` at 0.45 opacity | `var(--accent-orange-soft)` (= `rgba(212, 96, 42, 0.18)`) at 1.0 opacity → reads more orange-tinted-paper than orange-line |
| line quality | **solid** 0.5px stroke (96-segment smooth curve) | **dashed** 0.5px stroke (dash 4px / gap 3px) · OR particle (8 particles spaced along the curve, each 1.5px diameter, decreasing radius toward endpoint) — pick one per renderer build; SBA recommends dashed for v1.0 (cheaper geometry, more legible at small scale) |
| count | up to 45 edges (binding §3.3 density cap) | up to 5 tendrils per attended node (§3.3 cardinality) — *one* node at a time |
| persistence | active while attractor is non-`"all"` | active only while RW-5 drift is active on this node — fades on jump to next node |
| endpoint | real node glyph (full opacity highlight + 1.5px halo) | virtual coord with drift-signature halo (variants) · OR sibling node already highlighted by its own glyph (siblings) |

**The single-sentence test.** If a visitor watching the Globe says *"the network is humming"*, they are reading the binding edges. If they say *"that one node is dreaming"*, they are reading the branching tendrils. The mechanics must produce those two different sentences.

### 4.3 Coordinate model (Q7) · the math the prototype will need

The orbital shell is `R × 1.18` (ontology §4.2). The attended NeX node sits at world-space position `p_node` on that shell. Each tendril is a Bezier curve from `p_node` to an endpoint `p_endpoint`, with one control point `p_control`.

**Sibling tendrils (Channel B).**
- `p_endpoint` = the sibling node's derived orbital position (its own `(domain meridian + lateral drift, date latitude)` position on the shell).
- `p_control` = midpoint of `p_node` and `p_endpoint`, then lifted off the shell by `R × 0.03` (lifts the arc gently outward so it doesn't read as a chord through the shell · same gesture as binding §2.1 step 4 surface→orbit lifts).
- This gives a great-circle-ish arc on the outer side of the orbital shell, ~0.04 R above the shell at its apex.

**Variant tendrils (Channel A).**
Variants do not have real coordinates. They live at virtual positions derived from the variant's `alpha` value, projected outward from the attended node's position.

- `divergence_vector` = `p_node.normalize()` — the radial direction from origin through the node.
- `tangent_basis` = a pair of orthonormal vectors `(t_yaw, t_pitch)` perpendicular to `divergence_vector` (computed from world-up reference, stable per node).
- For variant `i` of `N` total variants on this node:
  - `angle_i` = `(2π × i / N) + phase_offset_node` — distributes variants evenly around the node's orbital tangent plane; `phase_offset_node` is a deterministic hash of `node.fileNum` so the layout is stable across reloads.
  - `delta_alpha_i` = `|variant.alpha − site.alpha|` — magnitude of divergence (always positive).
  - `distance_i` = `R × (0.06 + min(delta_alpha_i × 60, 0.10))` — distance from `p_node` to virtual endpoint, scaled by divergence magnitude. Clamped: closer variants pull tighter, farther variants reach further, never beyond `R × 0.16` outward.
  - `direction_i` = `t_yaw × cos(angle_i) + t_pitch × sin(angle_i)` — the tangent direction in the node's local frame.
  - `p_endpoint` = `p_node + direction_i × distance_i`.
- `p_control` = midpoint of `p_node` and `p_endpoint`, lifted by `R × 0.015` along `divergence_vector` (outward from globe center). This gives a gentle outward bow — the tendril curves *away* from the body, not toward it.

The result: each variant tendril is a short arc reaching outward from the attended node, in a direction determined by its index, at a distance determined by its divergence magnitude. They fan in a halo pattern around the node, not radially outward from globe center.

**Why not Lissajous trajectories** (brainstorm proposed parametric paths). Lissajous trajectories produce beautiful figures, but they require a parameter source that has no natural meaning in the data (tag overlap? content hash?). The divergence-vector projection is the simpler model that *reads* — distance encodes how-far-the-variant-is from this α, angle distributes legibly. Lissajous is logged as a future-extension option (§12 open questions).

### 4.4 Endpoint visual (drift-signature halo · variant tendrils only)

The variant endpoint is a small marker at `p_endpoint`:
- A 3px-diameter open ring (same glyph family as fiction nodes — hollow ring per ontology §4.5).
- Color: `var(--accent-orange-soft)` at 0.6 opacity (slightly more visible than the tendril line).
- The endpoint does NOT participate in hover/click in v1 (read-only).
- On reduced-motion: endpoint is rendered identically; no breathing.

The endpoint is the *drift-signature halo* of Q2 option C: the visitor reads it as "this node, but at α′ — a slightly different signature, in this direction".

### 4.5 Sibling tendril endpoint visual

No special marker — the sibling node is already a real NeX glyph at its own position. The tendril simply terminates at the sibling's position. The sibling glyph itself does NOT highlight or halo (that would conflate with binding's in-membership halo). The visitor reads: *the line went to that other node*. The sibling node remains visually quiet.

## 5 · attention trigger + dwell + fade (Q3)

### 5.1 Decision · option (i) on NETRA tracking · branches activate when RW-5 drift is active on a NeX node

The four candidates (Q3):

| option | trigger | adopted? |
|---|---|---|
| (i) on NETRA tracking (RW-5 drift active on NeX node) | jump-to-node + slerp arrive + drift starts | **YES** |
| (ii) on dwell (hover N ms) | mouse hover threshold | NO (hover is a separate input · would create two competing activation paths · also fails on touch) |
| (iii) on stratum focus (`3 · ORBIT` FRAMING button) | left-rail framing change | NO (would activate branches on every orbital node simultaneously · violates "one node at a time" reading) |
| (iv) on second click | click sequence semantics | NO (overloads the click affordance · contradicts journey-arch §2.3 click contract) |

**Why RW-5 tracking is the right trigger.**
Per `WorldlineGlobe.tsx` (iter 1 prototype line 2649ff) and `journey-architecture.md` §2.3, RW-5 is the *tracking-not-freezing* mechanic: when NETRA jumps to a node and drift activates, the camera maintains a parallax orbit around the target node at amplitude proportional to the node's stratum (NeX nodes get ±0.090 rad yaw, the most pronounced — 5× the surface amplitude). The TRACKING badge in the HUD top-right corner (`#hud-tracking-badge`, prototype line 1542) reads `TRACKING · <node-name>`.

This is the *moment of attention*. The visitor has chosen a NeX node, NETRA has flown to it, the camera is orbiting it. They are looking at one node. Branching activates exactly here — when the visitor's gaze and the body's drift have both committed to this one transmission. Branching ends exactly when the visitor commits to a different node (NEXT NODE press · click another pin · click a left-rail FRAMING button — all of which call `deactivateDrift()` in the prototype, per line 2429–2432).

### 5.2 Timing (matches RW-5 motion vocabulary, prototype line 2710ff)

| event | trigger | timing | easing |
|---|---|---|---|
| node clicked / NEXT NODE pressed | `setSelectedId(fileNum)` | n/a (state change) | — |
| camera slerp to node | already in flight from journey-arch §2.3 | 1100ms | `easeInOutCubic` |
| RW-5 drift activates after slerp settles | `__jumpAnimating === false` (prototype guard, line 2682) | drift fades in over 800ms (prototype `DRIFT_FADE_IN_MS`) | linear blend |
| **branches activate** | drift active + branches data present | begins 200ms after drift fade-in starts; tendrils draw with stroke-dash-offset animation 600ms; halo endpoints fade in 400ms | `easeOutQuad` for line draw; linear for halo alpha |
| NETRA voice line surfaces | branches activated | 100ms after first tendril completes draw | n/a |
| branches breathe with RW-5 drift | drift active, branches active | continuous, phase-locked to drift phase clock | — (see §7 RW-5 composition) |
| **branches deactivate** | `deactivateDrift()` called (NEXT NODE, framing change, click another node) | tendrils fade out 220ms (alpha → 0); halo endpoints fade 180ms | linear; halo finishes first so endpoint vanishes before line completes its retreat — reads as "drawing back into the node" |
| NETRA voice silences | tendrils dropped below 0.2 alpha | line ages out via the existing voice strip rhythm (no special handling) | — |

**No looping animation.** Branches do not pulse, throb, or rotate on their own clock. Their only motion is the RW-5 drift breathing (see §7). This honors I2 (Globe is body) — the body's imagining shares the body's breath; it does not have its own.

**Reduced-motion compensation.**
- Branches appear with instant alpha swap (no 600ms draw)
- Halo endpoints appear instantly
- No breathing with RW-5 drift (the drift itself is suppressed under reduced-motion per prototype reduced-motion respect — branches inherit)
- NETRA voice line surfaces normally

### 5.3 The visitor's reading

The visitor clicks a fiction node. Camera flies. Camera arrives. The slow drift begins. ~200ms in, three or four thin orange-paper-tinted dashed curves draw themselves outward from the node, ending in small open rings. NETRA's voice line in the bottom strip changes:

> `// netra · transmission t.001 holds three speculative orbits · α 1.129801 · 1.130892 · 1.131204.`

The lines breathe with the camera's drift. The visitor reads. They press NEXT NODE. The lines collapse back into the node and the camera leaves. The next node, if it has branches of its own, will bloom them in its own time.

## 6 · interaction model (Q4)

### 6.1 Decision · (b) read-only signal in v1.0 · (c) hybrid logged for v1.1

The three candidates (Q4):

| option | semantics | adopted? |
|---|---|---|
| (a) full affordance — tendril clickable, navigates to variant | each tendril is a path | NO — virtual coords have no destination page; would require creating "alternate-α variant" pages which doesn't exist in content layer yet (Procyon scope) |
| (b) read-only signal — branches are atmospheric | visual story only | **YES (v1.0)** |
| (c) hybrid — hover preview, click jumps | mid-ground | LOGGED for v1.1 once sibling-tendril click semantics are agreed with Peat (open question §12 Q-J) |

**v1.0 contract: branches are read-only.**
The visitor does NOT click tendrils. They do not hover-to-preview tendrils. The branches are atmospheric — they show possibility, they read instrument, they yield when the visitor moves on. Navigation continues to happen via:
- Click on a NeX node glyph → side panel (existing journey-arch §2.3)
- NEXT NODE button → jump to next worldline-curated node (existing)
- ChapterIndex card → entry page (existing)

The tendril's hit region in v1.0 is `null`. No pointer events. No keyboard focus. No screen-reader name.

**Why no interaction in v1.0.**
Three reasons:
1. **No destination for variant tendrils.** A variant tendril ends at a virtual coordinate, not a node. There is no page to navigate to. Building "alternate-α variant" pages is a content-layer decision Peat must make (§12 Q-A).
2. **Sibling tendril click would compete with the sibling node's own click.** If clicking a sibling tendril navigates to the sibling node, the visitor has two affordances for the same destination (the sibling node's own glyph already navigates there). Two affordances for one destination is the kind of redundancy that erodes the body's calm.
3. **The atmospheric-only register honors I3 (NETRA narrates, doesn't perform).** NETRA's job is to surface the variants in voice — that is the interaction. The visitor reads NETRA, looks at the lines, understands. Adding a click invitation would push branching toward a UI element. v1.0 keeps it as instrument signal.

**v1.1 hybrid logged.** Once Peat decides on variant-content treatment (§12 Q-A), the sibling tendril click can become a navigation affordance and variant tendril click can either remain inert or surface a side-panel preview of the variant's `delta_summary`. v1.1 spec will revisit.

### 6.2 Keyboard accessibility in v1.0

Branches are visual-only in v1.0. The NETRA voice line is `aria-live="polite"` (existing prototype convention, prototype line 1542), so a screen-reader user hears the activation as a voice update:

> `netra · transmission t.001 holds three speculative orbits · alpha 1.129801, 1.130892, 1.131204.`

The variant count and α values are read aloud. The tendril visuals are not focusable, not announced as separate elements. Screen-reader users get the same *information* (which variants exist, in which direction of α) through voice; they do not get the same *visual halo*. This is acceptable for v1.0 because the variants have no destination pages — there is nothing to navigate to.

When v1.1 introduces interaction, focusable proxy buttons per branch will follow the ontology §11.7 keyboard-exploration pattern (proxy buttons receive Tab focus; their focus drives the canvas focus ring).

## 7 · RW-5 drift composition (Q6)

### 7.1 Decision · option (c) branches breathe independently with phase-lock to RW-5

The three candidates (Q6):

| option | behavior | adopted? |
|---|---|---|
| (a) co-drift with camera | branches stay in node's frame, move with target | NO — visually equivalent to (c) but doesn't read as branches having their own breath |
| (b) static while camera drifts | branches in world space, camera orbits around them | NO — would induce parallax decoupling between node and branches; reads as branches detached |
| (c) breathe independently · phase-locked to drift | branches anchored to node, but tendril shape modulates with drift phase | **YES** |

**The mechanic.**
Each tendril has two parameters that modulate with the RW-5 drift phase clock (prototype `_driftPhaseT`, line 2704):

- **`p_control` lift amount** — the control-point offset along `divergence_vector` (currently `R × 0.015`) oscillates by ±15% with the same yaw frequency as the camera drift, but with a phase offset of π/4 (45°). This makes the tendril arc "puff" slightly outward and back as the camera orbits the node.
- **`p_endpoint` rotation around the node** — for variant tendrils, the endpoint orbits the node's tangent plane by ±0.012 rad (~0.7°) at the camera's pitch frequency. This is a very subtle azimuthal sway — the endpoint doesn't visibly move much, but it never holds perfectly still.

**Why phase-locked, not free-running.**
The RW-5 contract (prototype line 2649ff) says drift "reads as a telescope tracking mount following an off-center target." Branches sharing that phase means they read as part of the same observation — the body's whole attention has a single breath. Free-running branches would establish a *second* clock, and the visitor would notice the misalignment. One clock per body.

**Why ±15% / ±0.012 rad — calibration.**
The camera's NeX drift is ±0.090 rad yaw, ±0.055 rad pitch (prototype line 2666). The branches' modulation is approximately one order of magnitude smaller. The branches must *breathe with* the camera, not *match* it — the camera is doing the survey work; the branches are responding to that survey. If branches moved at the same amplitude as the camera, the visitor's eye would track them and lose the camera motion. The smaller amplitude lets the camera's drift remain the foreground motion.

**Motion budget audit.**
The RW-5 drift is the body's existing motion budget on NeX nodes. Branches add no new continuous motion — they ride on the existing motion's phase. This means no new nausea-risk vector is introduced. The amplitude is conservative.

**Sibling tendrils.**
Sibling endpoints are real positions of other NeX nodes. They DO NOT modulate. (Modulating them would move the sibling node away from its own position, which is wrong — the sibling has a fixed position in the orbital shell, and the tendril must terminate exactly there.) Only the control point's lift modulates for sibling tendrils — the line breathes, the endpoints stay fixed.

### 7.2 Drift fade-out

When the visitor presses NEXT NODE or clicks another node, `deactivateDrift()` runs. The branches:
- Decouple from the drift phase clock immediately (no more breathing)
- Fade out per §5.2 timing table

The fade-out begins *before* the camera slerp to the next node starts — the branches dissolve into the node and the camera leaves. The fade-in for the next node's branches (if any) starts after the slerp arrives and drift re-activates. There is never overlap between two nodes' branches.

## 9 · motion timing table · alpha discipline · token usage

### 9.1 Master timing table

| event | duration | easing | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| camera slerp to NeX node | 1100ms | easeInOutCubic | existing — journey-arch §2.3 |
| RW-5 drift fade-in | 800ms (linear blend) | linear | existing — prototype `DRIFT_FADE_IN_MS` line 2717 |
| branches activate (delay after drift starts) | +200ms | — | new |
| tendril stroke-dash-offset draw-in | 600ms | easeOutQuad | new |
| variant endpoint halo fade-in | 400ms | linear | new (begins with tendril draw, finishes earlier) |
| NETRA voice line surfaces | tendril complete + 100ms | — | rides existing voice strip rhythm |
| branches breathing (phase-locked to drift) | continuous, phase-shifted π/4 from camera | — | new |
| branches fade-out on deactivateDrift | 220ms (line) / 180ms (halo) | linear | new |
| reduced-motion: all of the above | instant or 0ms | — | matches existing reduced-motion convention |

**Total time from click to fully-bloomed branches: ~1900ms** (1100 slerp + 800 drift fade-in starts at ~1100, branches activate at 1100+200 = 1300, complete draw at 1300+600 = 1900). The visitor sees roughly 2 seconds between click and fully-rendered branches. This is intentionally slow — branches are the destination of attention, not a UI response. Compare: the side panel opens immediately on click (existing behavior). Branches earn their bloom.

### 9.2 Alpha discipline

| element | alpha | notes |
|---|---|---|
| tendril line at rest (drift active, branches in steady state) | 0.18 (= `var(--accent-orange-soft)` opacity baseline) | dashed stroke; against paper, reads as faint orange ink |
| tendril line during breathing apex (control-point puff outward) | 0.22 | momentary, +0.04 above baseline |
| tendril line during breathing trough (control-point inward) | 0.14 | momentary, -0.04 below baseline |
| variant endpoint ring | 0.6 | brighter than the tendril; the endpoint is where the eye lands |
| sibling endpoint (real node glyph) | unchanged | renders at its own opacity per ontology §7.1 |

**Why alpha is the primary discipline, not stroke weight.**
The attractor-binding edges are 0.5px solid stroke at 0.45 opacity. The worldline branches are also 0.5px stroke, but dashed and at 0.18 opacity. The stroke widths match — what differs is line *quality* (dashed vs solid) and *alpha* (much fainter). The two mechanics share scale; they differ in vibe. This is the iter-1 lesson: keep the geometric vocabulary tight; vary alpha and pattern to differentiate phenomena. (See `09-article-entry.md` v2.0 §reading-mode token discipline — the same logic applied to a different surface.)

### 9.3 Token usage

| region | token |
|---|---|
| tendril line color | `var(--accent-orange-soft)` (= `rgba(212, 96, 42, 0.18)` per `globals.css` line 42) |
| tendril line stroke (dashed) | `1px dashed var(--accent-orange-soft)` — the dashed quality is the visual differentiator from binding's solid arcs |
| variant endpoint ring color | `var(--accent-orange)` (= `#D4602A` per `globals.css` line 41) at 0.6 opacity (applied via material alpha, not new token) |
| NETRA voice line color | unchanged — `var(--netra)` per existing convention |
| HUD TRACKING badge background during branching-active | unchanged — RW-5 already styles this |

**No new tokens introduced.** The two existing accent-orange tokens (`--accent-orange` and `--accent-orange-soft`) carry the full mechanic. This passes the token-discipline gate. If the renderer needs a third tier of orange between the two, propose via Polaris (TOKEN PROPOSAL handoff per agent contract), do not invent inline.

**Why not introduce a `--branch-orange` semantic token.**
A semantic token would make sense if branches appeared on many surfaces in many contexts. They appear in exactly one place (NeX shell when RW-5 drift active on a node). A new token at v1.0 would be over-naming. If branches earn a second surface in a future feature, revisit.

## 10 · accessibility · reduced-motion stories

### 10.1 Screen-reader story

A visually impaired visitor navigates the Globe via the proxy-button system (ontology §11.7). They Tab to a NeX node. They press Enter. The screen reader announces:

> "Transmission t-zero-zero-one, fiction, NeX orbital, in domain reflection."

The camera flies (silent to screen reader; visual only). Drift activates. Branches bloom. The NETRA voice strip is `aria-live="polite"`, so the screen reader announces:

> "Transmission t-zero-zero-one holds three speculative orbits at alpha one point one two nine eight zero one, one point one three zero eight nine two, one point one three one two zero four."

The variant alphas are read aloud. The visitor presses NEXT NODE. The screen reader announces the next node's identity and any branches it has.

The screen-reader user gets the same *information* (variants exist, in which alpha directions) through voice. They do not see the orbital halo, but they hear the count and the directions. The variants have no destination pages in v1.0, so no navigation is lost.

### 10.2 Reduced-motion story

A visitor with `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` sees:
- The Globe itself respects reduced-motion (existing — idle drift suppressed per ontology §6.4)
- RW-5 camera drift is suppressed (existing)
- Branches: when the visitor clicks a NeX node, the slerp may still happen (existing UX decision; jumps are bounded purposeful motion, not idle decoration); on arrival, the branches appear with INSTANT alpha swap (no 600ms draw, no 400ms halo fade). The variant endpoints appear instantly. The NETRA voice line surfaces normally.
- Branches do NOT breathe with RW-5 drift (since drift is suppressed under reduced-motion, the question is moot)
- On deactivation: branches disappear with instant alpha swap

The visitor still gets the information layer: which variants exist, where they fan around the node, what NETRA says about them. They are spared the continuous breathing motion.

### 10.3 Hit-target story (touch / keyboard)

In v1.0, branches have no hit targets. The picking radius is 0. There is nothing to tab to within the branch cloud. The NeX node itself remains the only focusable element (proxy button per ontology §11.7 / §11.8). When v1.1 introduces interaction (per §6.1), each branch endpoint will become a proxy button with a minimum 44px hit region (WCAG 2.5.5), placed at the endpoint's screen-space projection.

### 10.4 Color contrast story

The tendril line at 0.18 alpha against `var(--paper-base)` (`#E8E2D5`) does NOT meet WCAG 3:1 for graphical elements when measured as a thin dashed line. This is acceptable for *decorative graphical elements* — the tendril is not conveying information that is unavailable elsewhere. The information (which variants exist, at which alpha) is fully conveyed through the NETRA voice line (aria-live), the visible endpoint halos (`var(--accent-orange)` at 0.6 — passes 3:1 against paper-base), and the side-panel content (when sibling click is added in v1.1).

The variant endpoint halos at 0.6 alpha give the necessary visual anchor. The tendril line is the connective tissue between node and halo; if the line is faint, the halo and node still establish the visual relationship.

## 12 · open questions for Peat

### Q-A · variant content treatment

**Question.** Each variant tendril has a `delta_summary` string in the fiction frontmatter. Does the variant have a *destination page* (so v1.1 can make sibling tendrils clickable in parity, with variant tendrils navigating to a "variant view" of the parent fiction at that α)?

Three possible answers:
- (A1) Variants are voice-only — they exist as `delta_summary` strings narrated by NETRA, and that's it. No pages, no interaction. Branching stays read-only forever.
- (A2) Variants get a *variant overlay* — same fiction page, but the visible body shifts to a variant-conditioned version (different paragraphs, different ending, different structural beats — content authored per variant). The overlay is reached via a future variant-affordance, possibly tendril click in v1.1.
- (A3) Variants are full alternate pages — each variant has its own URL, full body, separate edit history. The fiction node branches to N pages, each a full transmission at that α.

This is a content-layer scope decision. It cascades to interaction model (Q4), authoring burden, and the v1.1 spec. I lean (A1) for v1.0 simplicity, with (A2) as the natural growth path if Peat wants more depth later. ### 13.1 Data layer (Procyon scope — schema)

Add to the existing fiction-entry frontmatter (per `lib/entries.ts`):

```yaml

  - alpha: 1.131204
```

**Schema rules:**
- `variants[*].alpha` is a string (preserving precision · same convention as site α `"1.130426"`)
- `variants[*].delta_summary` is a sentence-case prose string, max 120 chars (NETRA reads it; if it's longer, NETRA truncates to first clause)
- `divergence_cluster` is a kebab-case string label, max 40 chars; pre-computed sibling sets are derived at build time

**Empty-state handling:**
- Missing `variants` → no variant tendrils for this node
- Missing `divergence_cluster` → no sibling tendrils for this node
- Both missing → no branching available; NETRA voice line acknowledges (§3.3 empty-state line)

This is a SCHEMA NEEDS handoff to Procyon, surfaced here for Polaris routing.

### 13.2 Renderer (Sirius scope — Three.js)

The renderer additions, all on the existing `WorldlineGlobe.tsx` (or its v1.3 successor):

1. **At scene init.** Mount a `branchesGroup` as a child of the orbital scene group. Initially empty. Z-order: above orbital shell mesh, below attractor-binding edges (so branches read as on-orbital-shell, but binding's network can sit on top when both are active — which it shouldn't normally, but in the edge case, binding takes visual priority since it spans the body).

2. **On RW-5 drift activation.** Read the attended node's `variants` and `divergence_cluster` (precomputed sibling list). Compute branch geometry per §4.3. Instantiate Bezier curves (2 control points sufficient for the gentle bow) with `THREE.LineDashedMaterial` for dashed quality. Add endpoint meshes for variant endpoints (small open-ring sprites, 3px diameter, sharing existing fiction-node ring sprite material with adjusted opacity).

3. **Tendril draw-in animation.** Animate each curve's `material.dashOffset` from `dashLength × totalLength` to `0` over 600ms (easeOutQuad). This produces the "drawing itself" effect.

4. **Halo endpoint fade-in.** Animate each endpoint sprite's material opacity from 0 to 0.6 over 400ms (linear).

5. **Phase-locked breathing.** In the existing RAF loop (prototype `loop()`), for each active branch:
   - Read `_driftPhaseT` (existing).
   - Compute control-point lift modulation per §7.1 (±15% based on yaw phase + π/4 offset).
   - For variant tendrils, compute endpoint sway (±0.012 rad on pitch phase).
   - Update curve geometry (cheap — only one control point moves per tendril; endpoint moves per variant). Use `Curve.getPoints()` re-sample sparingly (cache when phase delta < 0.01 rad).

6. **On `deactivateDrift()`.** Fade out all tendrils (220ms) and endpoints (180ms). After fade complete, dispose curve geometry and remove from scene. (Memory hygiene — branches are short-lived; do not retain across nodes.)

7. **NETRA voice update.** Use existing `aria-live` voice-strip mechanism. Push the activation line ~100ms after first tendril draw completes. Push the deactivation revert (back to default stratum voice line) when fade-out completes.

8. **Reduced-motion branch.** Honor `matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)').matches` per existing convention. Skip the draw-in animation; set material opacity to target value instantly. Skip the breathing modulation; render at baseline geometry.
